## 0. Method and limits (read first)

- **Network limit:** In this research session, direct page fetches of `adsterra.com` and `intercom.help` (Adsterra's publisher FAQ host) **failed**. The hosts were outside the session's allowed network. All findings come from web-search result snippets of those pages. They are not full-page reads. Anything marked "Adsterra (own)" is Adsterra's own content as quoted in a search snippet. **Before relying on any of it, re-read it in a browser or ask the Adsterra personal manager.**
- **Source labels:** Each source is tagged **[Adsterra]** (Adsterra's own page), **[Official]** (another network's or a regulator's own documentation) or **[3rd-party]** (reviews, blogs, affiliates). Third-party claims about Adsterra are weak evidence. Many review sites are themselves Adsterra affiliates.
- **Dates:** All URLs were accessed on 2026-11-14. Where a page shows no publication date in the snippet, it says "date not shown".

## 1. Summary: what can be verified

| Control | Documented by Adsterra itself? | How it is done | Guaranteed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mainstream-only vs. "all ads" (adult / non-mainstream)** | **Yes, partly.** Adsterra's blog describes picking a traffic type (mainstream or non-mainstream) and an "accept all ads" toggle (also called "BOOST CPM") when you request a code | Self-serve, in the dashboard, when you create a code or Smartlink | No guarantee language found |
| **Excluding specific ad types / feeds** (third parties name Erotic, Gambling, Software, Alert, Sound ads) | **Partly.** Adsterra says you can "get in touch with Adsterra managers and ask to exclude ad types". The named category list comes **only from third-party tutorials** | Request to the **personal manager**. Some third parties describe a toggle list when a Direct Link is created | No guarantee language found |
| **Gambling / betting as a separate block** | **Not verified.** No Adsterra page found that names a gambling-only switch for publishers | Ask the manager. A third-party tutorial lists "Gambling Ads" as an excludable feed | Not verifiable |
| **Dating** | **Not verified.** Adsterra's advertiser guides class dating as "non-mainstream" | Probably covered by "mainstream only", but this is not confirmed | Not verifiable |
| **Crypto / finance** | **Not verified.** No publisher-side control found | Ask the manager | Not verifiable |
| **Software downloads / "download"-style ads** | **Partly.** Adsterra says it bans malware and "unsolicited downloads". "Software Ads" appears as an excludable feed only in third-party tutorials | Ask the manager | Not verifiable |
| **Blocking a specific advertiser / landing domain** | **Not verified for publishers.** Adsterra's blacklisting docs are written for **advertisers** (placement and keyword blacklists) | Ask the manager with the landing URL or screenshot (inferred, not documented) | Not verifiable |
| **Per-country controls for publishers** (e.g. no gambling in country X) | **Not found** | No documented publisher-side geo-category setting | Not verifiable |

**Bottom line:** From Adsterra's own content, the only control that can reasonably be called documented is the **mainstream vs. all-ads (non-mainstream/adult) choice** at code creation. Adsterra also confirms in general terms that **managers can exclude ad types** on request. Nothing found shows that **gambling/betting, dating, crypto/finance or software-download** blocking is a documented, self-serve or guaranteed control. Nothing found shows **per-country** category controls for publishers. Treat all of these as **"request in writing from the personal manager, then check by observation"**, not as enforceable settings.

### 2.3 Is blocking "guaranteed" or "best effort"?

- **No Adsterra statement was found that guarantees** exclusion of any category. Adsterra's own wording is advisory. It recommends allowing all ads for CPM and invites you to *ask* managers to exclude ad types.
- Self-declared categories (advertisers labelling their own campaigns as mainstream or not) plus manual or algorithmic moderation are, by their nature, best effort. **Assume misclassified creatives can get through.**
- **Recommended practice:** get the exclusion list confirmed **in writing** (email or ticket) from the personal manager. Run a test period with logging (screenshots and landing URLs by country). Keep a kill switch. YoniXTools already gates ads behind a suppression flag.

### 2.4 Questions to send to the Adsterra personal manager (copy/paste)

1. For our zones/codes, please confirm **mainstream-only** is set and the "accept all ads / BOOST CPM" option is **off**.
2. Please exclude these feeds/categories and confirm each one in writing: **gambling/betting/casino/sweepstakes, adult/erotic/cams, dating, crypto/trading/forex/"get rich", software/browser-extension/"download" and fake alerts, ads with sound**.
3. Is the exclusion applied **globally** for our account or **per zone**? Is there any **per-country** override?
4. Is exclusion **guaranteed** or best effort? What is the process and turnaround to report and block a specific advertiser or landing domain?
5. Do our pages (browser tools + public-post social downloader) comply with the Publisher Terms "third-party rights / copyright" clause? Please confirm in writing.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Keep ads suppressed** until the Adsterra manager confirms in writing: mainstream-only, BOOST/"all ads" off, and exclusion of gambling, adult, dating, crypto/finance and software/alert/sound feeds (see §2.4).
2. **Treat any confirmation as best effort.** Before going live, add a monitoring routine: weekly screenshots by locale/country, and a way for users to report an ad.
3. **Consider splitting monetisation by section.** Downloader pages carry the highest policy risk with Google-family networks. Tool pages (PDF/image/QR/text) are lower risk and could later qualify for AdSense or Ezoic, which have **documented self-serve** category blocking.
4. **Re-run this research with network access** to `adsterra.com` and `intercom.help` to read the full publisher FAQ and terms. Record the exact version dates.
