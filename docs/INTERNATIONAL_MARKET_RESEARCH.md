## 0. Method, rules and limits

- **Numbers:** Every figure has a source name, URL and date. Internet-user figures come from the **DataReportal / Kepios "Digital 2026"** country reports. That series was published in late 2025 and its figures refer to **October 2025**. (DataReportal says the Digital 2026 series "relies on data up to October 2025": https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-global-overview-report.) Search results also surfaced "Digital 2027" pages that claim end-2026 figures. Today is 2026-11-14, so those were **not used**; they could not be checked and may be projections.
- **Search engine shares** are from **StatCounter Global Stats**, with the month stated. StatCounter values differ by view (all platforms, mobile, desktop) and by month. They measure page-view referrals, not query volume.
- **Tooling limit:** Direct page fetching was blocked in this session. Figures were taken from search-result excerpts of the cited pages. A spot-check against the live pages is recommended before external use.
- **Internet population ≠ demand.** Large internet populations show *potential reach* only. They do not show demand for PDF, image, video or downloader tools. Where no category evidence was found, the text says **"no evidence found"**. Competitor audience estimates (Semrush/Ahrefs) are third-party models of *competitors'* traffic. They are directional only and are **not** measurements of demand for YoniXTools.
- All URLs were accessed 2026-11-14.

## 3. Competition (qualitative; no traffic numbers invented)

Well-known competitors exist in every target market. Most of them are themselves multilingual:
- **PDF:** iLovePDF, Smallpdf, PDF24, Sejda, PDF2Go, PDF Candy, Adobe Acrobat online. iLovePDF and Smallpdf offer many interface languages, so localization alone is not a differentiator.
- **Image:** TinyPNG/TinyJPG, iLoveIMG, Squoosh (Google), Compressor.io, Canva/Adobe Express (resize and crop).
- **QR:** QR Code Generator (qr-code-generator.com), QR Code Monkey, plus many local sites.
- **Downloaders:** SnapTik, SSSTik, SaveFrom, SnapInsta, FDown-type sites, plus many short-lived clone domains (Ahrefs lists e.g. snaptik.sbs, savefrom.space). This segment is crowded, has low barriers to entry, and carries policy and copyright risk (see the Adsterra doc, §3).
- **China:** global tools may be slow or unreachable. Local alternatives are common (e.g. WPS, plus Baidu-indexed domestic tool sites). **Not researched in depth.**
- **Korea / Japan / Russia:** portal ecosystems (Naver, Yahoo! Japan, Yandex) have their own services and SERP features. Ranking dynamics differ from Google-only markets.

## 4. Monetization (display ads)

- **No authoritative, published per-country CPM table was found.** Adsterra's own blog says Tier-1 countries "usually pay (and cost) more" and advises publishers to check country CPM "with your ad network manager" (https://adsterra.com/blog/what-is-a-good-cpm ; https://adsterra.com/blog/geos-with-high-cpm-rates-for-publishers ; dates not shown). Third-party blog ranges exist, but they are not reliable enough to plan with (see the Adsterra doc, §2.2).
- **Qualitative (industry convention, not a measured source):** US, UK, DE, FR, NL, JP, KR and Nordic markets are usually treated as higher-value "Tier 1". BR, MX, TR, ZA and Eastern Europe are usually treated as "Tier 2". IN, ID, PK, BD, NG, EG, VN, PH and KE are usually treated as "Tier 3", meaning lower CPM but high volume.
- **Consequence:** high-volume / low-CPM markets (hi, id, bn, ur) can bring traffic but little revenue per user. Lower-volume / higher-CPM markets (de, fr, nl, ja) can earn more per visit.
- **Local payment methods are not needed.** The tools are free and earn through ads.

## 5. Advertising and data-protection restrictions

### 5.1 Gambling advertising

The per-country gambling-advertising table (with sources) is in **`docs/i18n/ADSTERRA_CONTROLS_RESEARCH.md` §4**. Short version: gambling ads are **illegal or effectively banned** in markets including **India (online money games, Act of 2025), China, Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Thailand, Vietnam (except state products), Japan (online casino ads banned from Sept 2025) and Türkiye (largely)**. They are **regulated / licensed** in the US (by state), UK, Germany, France (no online casino), Brazil, Mexico, Kenya, Nigeria (by state), the Philippines and South Africa. **Recommendation: block the gambling category globally.** ⚖️ All of this needs legal verification.

### 5.3 Other legal notes ⚖️

- **Downloader section:** Copyright and platform-ToS exposure differs by country. Google publisher policies restrict pages that enable downloading of streaming content where the provider prohibits it (see the Adsterra doc, §3). Consider keeping the downloader out of some markets' SEO focus, and keep the "public posts only / respect rights" messaging localized.
- **China:** Serving the mainland reliably usually requires mainland hosting, which requires an ICP licence. Content may be blocked by the Great Firewall. Google services (Fonts, reCAPTCHA, AdSense) can fail there. ⚖️/technical verification needed.
- **Russia:** Sanctions, payment-rail and ad-network availability issues. Yandex dominates search. Not researched in depth.

## 7. Prioritized recommendation

Reasoning criteria, in order: (1) **search-engine fit** (Google-dominant markets reward the site's existing SEO); (2) **evidence of category demand** (§2, weak but present); (3) **internet population** (reach, not demand); (4) **monetization quality** (qualitative, §4); (5) **regulatory/technical friction** (§5).

**Tier 1 (launch first, invest in native-quality translations and localized SEO pages):**
- **en**: the base. Covers US, UK, IN, PH, NG, ZA and KE. US/UK are higher-value ad markets; SnapTik's audience estimate includes the US.
- **es**: Mexico (110 M users) plus Spain, US-Hispanic and LatAm. Google-dominant; one locale covers many countries.
- **pt-BR**: Brazil (185 M users). Brazil appears in both competitor-audience signals (iLovePDF, SnapTik). Google-dominant.
- **id**: Indonesia (≈230 M users). Indonesia appears in both competitor-audience signals and is #1 for SnapTik/SaveFrom search in the third-party estimates. Google-dominant. Low CPM expected.
- **hi**: India (1.03 B users; Google ≈98%). India is the top iLovePDF audience per Semrush. Caveat: much Indian search is in English, so en is equally important for India. Low CPM expected.

**Tier 2 (launch after Tier 1 is stable; good monetization or large reach, Google-dominant):**
- **fr, de, it, nl**: smaller populations but higher-value ad markets (qualitative). GDPR consent is mandatory. nl is the smallest; it could drop to Tier 3 if translation budget is tight.
- **ja**: 107 M users and a higher-value market. Google leads, but Bing/Yahoo! are significant, and Bing Webmaster submission matters.
- **tr**: 77.5 M users; Google ≈80%, with Yandex secondary.
- **vi**: 85.6 M users; Google ≈93% (third-party).
- **ar**: Egypt 98.2 M plus Saudi Arabia 34.4 M plus MENA. Needs RTL engineering, which is a one-time cost that is reusable for ur.
- **bn, ur**: large reach (Bangladesh 82.8 M; Pakistan large), Google-dominant, but low CPM expected. Ur also needs RTL (reuses ar work).

**Tier 3 (localize UI, but expect weaker organic return until validated):**
- **ru**: Yandex ≈71% means separate SEO work, plus sanctions/ad-network/payment uncertainty and 152-FZ.
- **ko**: Naver ≈69% on mobile; Naver-specific SEO is required.
- **zh-CN**: Baidu-dominant, Google inaccessible, possible blocking and ICP issues; downloader targets (TikTok international, Instagram, X, etc.) are themselves blocked in the mainland. zh-CN may still serve Chinese speakers in Malaysia/Singapore/overseas.
- **th**: 67.8 M users and Google-dominant, but no category evidence was found, and Thai line-breaking needs extra QA. It could move up to Tier 2 after validation.
- **sw**: Kenya 23.4 M + Tanzania. Small reach, and many users are served by en. Keep it as a goodwill/coverage locale.

**Explicitly not claimed:** No market is claimed to have the "highest demand" for any feature. The Tier 1 choice rests on Google fit, internet population, and the weak third-party competitor-audience signals listed in §2.

## 8. Validate after launch

The site **currently has no analytics**. Validation therefore has to start with tools that need no client tracking, and add privacy-respecting analytics later.

1. **Google Search Console** (no client script needed): verify the domain; use the *Performance → Countries* and *Pages* reports filtered by locale path (e.g. `/es/`, `/pt-br/`); track impressions, clicks, CTR and position per country and locale weekly for the first 8–12 weeks. Check *International targeting / hreflang* errors.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools** (important for JP, where Bing ≈33%), **Yandex Webmaster** (RU, TR), **Naver Search Advisor** (KR), **Baidu Ziyuan** (CN; may require a Chinese phone number, not verified).
3. **Server-side / edge logs** (no cookies): count requests per locale path and the `Accept-Language` distribution. For the downloader API, log successful vs. failed extractions per platform and per country (coarse, not personal), so that "operations completed" can be measured.
4. **Privacy-friendly analytics** (e.g. a cookieless, self-hosted tool) to count **successful tool operations** per tool × locale, e.g. "PDF merged", "image compressed", "download succeeded". Do this only with a consent design that works under §5.2. Because processing is client-side, operation counts need an explicit (anonymous) event.
5. **Ad performance by country** (once ads are re-enabled): Adsterra dashboard CPM/fill by GEO; compare against translation cost per locale.
6. **Decision rule (suggested):** after 90 days, promote a Tier 2/3 locale if its Search Console impressions and successful operations per week beat the median Tier 1 locale per translation-hour invested. Demote or freeze locales with near-zero impressions after indexation is confirmed.
7. **Google Trends checks** (not done in this research): compare relative interest for terms such as "compress pdf", "merge pdf", "tiktok downloader", "qr code generator" in each locale's language and country, to refine priorities.
